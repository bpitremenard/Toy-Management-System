# Architecture Decision Records (ADRs) — Overview

## What is an ADR?
An **Architecture Decision Record (ADR)** is a short document that captures an important technical decision made during a project, ensuring that both current and future team members understand the reasoning behind the system’s architecture.

> Code shows what was built.  
> ADRs explain why it was built that way.

---

## Purpose
ADRs are used to document:
- The **context** or problem that required a decision  
- The **options** that were considered  
- The **final decision** that was made  
- The **rationale** behind the decision  
- The **consequences** of that decision  

---

## Why ADRs are Important
Without ADRs, important decisions can become unclear over time. Future developers may:
- Revisit decisions that were already carefully evaluated  
- Introduce inconsistencies by unknowingly reversing prior choices  
- Lack understanding of the system’s design constraints  

By maintaining ADRs, teams can:
- Preserve **technical context**  
- Improve **communication and knowledge sharing**  
- Support **long-term maintainability** of the system
